# Privacy Policy

[← Back to Documentation](./index.md)

**Last updated:** set when published

This Privacy Policy explains how Ludo Plus ("the App") handles information when you use the App.

The App comes in two versions:

- **The Google Play version**, installed from Google Play. It shows an advertising banner, offers optional purchases, and
    lets you sign in to Google Play Games.
- **The GitHub version**, downloaded from the App's GitHub page. It has no advertising, no purchases, and no sign-in, and
    no permission to use the internet.

Where this policy says something applies to one version only, it says so.

## 1. Information We Collect

We (the developer of the App) do not run any servers, and we do not receive any information about you from the App.

The App does not ask you for personal information such as your name, email address, postal address, telephone number,
or payment information. It has no accounts of its own.

The App lets you type in names for the players in a game. These names are stored on your device (see section 2). If you
sign in to Google Play Games, they are also saved in your Google Play Games cloud save (see section 4).

## 2. Information Stored on Your Device

The App stores on your device:

- Your settings and preferences, and the players and game modes you last set up
- The game in progress, if there is one, so you can continue it
- Your finished games: the player names, places, turns, captures, and times, which make up the App's Leaderboard and
    Statistics
- Your achievements, and the time you have played
- Whether you have bought an optional purchase (the Google Play version)

This information is used only to operate the game and provide its features. You can clear it by clearing the App's data
or uninstalling it.

If you have enabled Android's backup feature on your device, Android may include the App's locally stored data in your
device backup (for example, to your Google account). This backup is managed by Android and Google under your device and
account settings, not by the App, and you can turn it off in your device's settings.

In the GitHub version, you can export your progress to a file and import it on another device. The file is saved where
you choose and is never sent anywhere by the App.

## 3. Advertising (the Google Play version)

The Google Play version shows an advertising banner at the bottom of most screens (never over a game in play). The ads
are provided by Google AdMob, a service of Google LLC.

To show and measure ads, AdMob may collect and use information from your device, such as your device's advertising ID,
IP address, and information about your device and your interactions with the ads. This is done by Google, under Google's
Privacy Policy (<https://policies.google.com/privacy>) and its explanation of how Google uses information from apps that
use its services (<https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites>).

Where the law requires it (for example, in the United Kingdom and the European Economic Area), the App asks for your
consent through Google's consent form before personalised ads are shown. You can change your choices at any time from
the App's Settings ("Ad privacy choices"). You can also reset or delete your device's advertising ID in your device's
settings.

You can remove the ads altogether with the optional "Remove ads" purchase (see section 5).

The App is not directed at children under 13, and its ads are not intended for them.

The GitHub version shows no ads and contains no advertising code.

## 4. Google Play Games (the Google Play version)

Signing in to Google Play Games is optional, and only happens when you choose Sign in in the App's Settings. When you
are signed in:

- Your achievements are sent to Google Play Games, and your fastest wins to its leaderboards.
- Your progress (your settings, the game in progress, your finished games, including the player names you typed, and
    your achievements) is saved to your Google Play Games cloud save, so you can continue on another device.
- The App shows your Google Play Games name and picture in its Settings.

This information is held by Google under Google's Privacy Policy. You can sign out in the App's Settings. You can delete
the App's data from Google Play Games in your Play Games profile or your Google Account.

## 4a. Playing With Phones Nearby (the Google Play version)

You can play one game across several phones in the same room, using Google's Nearby Connections. It finds and links
the phones directly, over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, with no server in between. To do so the App asks, when you first host or
join such a game, for permission to find and connect to nearby devices (on older versions of Android, location
permission, which Android requires for this; the App doesn't use your location).

While you host or look for a game, your phone shows the player name you set up to phones nearby running the App. In a
game, the phones send each other the game itself: its players' names and the moves. If a phone drops out of a game, the
host's phone shows its game to phones nearby again until that phone is back. Nothing is sent to us or kept anywhere but
on the phones in the game.

## 5. Purchases (the Google Play version)

The App offers optional one-time purchases ("Unlock all styles", "Remove ads", and both together). Purchases are made
through Google Play, which handles your payment details under its own terms and Google's Privacy Policy. The App only
learns whether you own a purchase, and keeps that on your device; we never see your payment information.

Every style in the App can also be earned for free through play.

## 6. Analytics and Crash Reports

The App does not use analytics services to track how you use it.

If the App crashes or stops responding, Google Play may collect technical diagnostic information (such as crash reports)
and share it with us in a form that does not identify you, as described in Google's Privacy Policy. This depends on your
device's settings. The Google Play Games and AdMob software in the Google Play version may also collect diagnostic
information about their own operation, for Google.

If the App stops because of an error, it shows a page describing the problem, with the option to report it on GitHub. The
App sends nothing by itself. Choosing to report opens your web browser at a new GitHub issue, filled in with:

- The App's version, and how it was installed
- Your device's manufacturer and model, its Android version, and its screen size and font size
- The language in use
- The App's recent actions, such as the pages opened and the moves made, which never include the player names you typed
- The technical details of the error

You can read and change all of it before posting, and nothing is sent unless you post it, which needs a GitHub account of
your own. GitHub issues are public, and GitHub's own terms and privacy statement apply to them. To have a report removed,
contact us (see section 10). You can also copy these details from the page to share them another way.

## 7. Third-Party Services

The Google Play version uses these Google services, each under Google's Privacy Policy and terms:

- Google AdMob and Google's consent form (advertising, section 3)
- Google Play Games Services (optional sign-in, section 4)
- Google Play Billing (optional purchases, section 5)
- Google Nearby Connections (playing with phones nearby, section 4a)

The GitHub version uses no third-party online services.

## 8. Children's Privacy

The App is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children, and the
App sends nothing to us about anyone who plays it.

## 9. Changes to This Privacy Policy

This Privacy Policy may be updated from time to time, particularly when new features or third-party services are
introduced. The updated policy will be made available at the same URL as this Privacy Policy, and the "Last updated" date
will be changed accordingly.
